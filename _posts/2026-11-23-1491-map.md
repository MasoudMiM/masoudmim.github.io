I finished Charles C. Mann's *1491: New Revelations of the Americas Before Columbus*. The book left me with the same feeling every good history book does, which was the urge to pin the whole thing to a wall. The book moves from the Mississippi mounds of Cahokia to the raised fields of the Amazon, from Inca road logistics to Maya drought politics. To me, every chapter was a torrent of place names. I kept flipping back to the maps in the front of the book, so I did what any data scientist would do, building my own map of places.

The map below shows every place Mann mentions *for a reason*, 1,129 locations across all 24 chapters, color-coded by what kind of place it is.

---

Chapters are grouped under the book's actual three parts. Further, under each chapter title, a small **stacked bar** shows how many places that chapter contributes (bar length) and what kinds of places they are (colors matching the legend). Chapter 9 on Amazonia and Chapter 10's long appendix trails look very different. You can click a dot for its story, hover for the name, or search finds places across all chapters. Same place mentioned twice in one chapter (Plymouth and Patuxet, for instance) merges into one dot with both names.
